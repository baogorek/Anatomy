Sources & model credits

SplineFitness Movement Lab is a non-commercial experimentation project built on the work of anatomy researchers, model authors and open-source contributors. The sources below identify what we use, where it came from and what we changed. Their authors do not endorse SplineFitness.

Publications document the source models and their studied tasks. Movement Lab calculates geometric muscle–tendon path lengths; its sliders and longest-path search do not establish tissue tension, activation, a personalized stretch or a safe range of motion. The resting atlas depicts a separate body and does not deform with the moving model.

Shoulder and deltoid study

Thoracoscapular model by A. Seth, M. Dong, R. Matias and S. Delp. Subject-scaled model, geometry and movement data distributed by Italo Belli, Sagar Joshi, J. Micah Prendergast, Irene Beck, Cosimo Della Santina, Luka Peternel and Ajay Seth (2023).

Used here. Regional Shoulder uses 33 model compartments and the supplied ABD01, FLX01 and SHRUG01 inverse-kinematics tracks. The deltoid close-up uses sampled native poses from the same model.

Our changes. The source model and meshes are preserved. We convert geometry for browser display, expose selected native joint controls, and calculate paths and lengths with OpenSim. Recorded tracks supply joint coordinates. Movement Lab does not run the paper’s muscle-redundancy solver or estimate its muscle forces. Deltoid anatomy is extracted separately from the resting atlas.

  • Belli I, Joshi S, Prendergast JM, Beck I, Della Santina C, Peternel L, Seth A. (2023). Does enforcing glenohumeral joint stability matter? A new rapid muscle redundancy solver highlights the importance of non-superficial shoulder muscles. PloS one 18(11): e0295003. DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0295003

Hip, knee and ankle

Lai–Uhlrich 2022 model distributed by the OpenCap team, with model lineage from Apoorva Rajagopal and colleagues and Adrian Lai, Allison Arnold and James Wakeling. Bone display geometry comes from the OpenSim models collection, including its Rajagopal geometry.

Used here. Regional Hip, Knee and Ankle share the model’s 40 right-side lower-limb muscle compartments. The OpenCap video-processing service is not used.

Our changes. Source model and mesh files are preserved. We select the right-side workspace, expose hip/knee/ankle/subtalar coordinates and convert source meshes for the browser. Unexposed joints retain the source’s defaults and coupling relationships. Whole body uses a different model, cited below.

Elbow, forearm and wrist

MoBL-ARMS by Katherine Saul, Xiao Hu, Craig Goehler, Meghan Vidt, Melissa Daly, Anca Velisar and Wendy Murray; subsequent work by D. C. McFarland, E. M. McCain, M. N. Poppo and K. R. Saul. Retrieved from the public Auctus/Inria model collection.

Used here. Regional Elbow and Wrist expose 17 selected muscle paths from the MoBL-ARMS fixed-41 model. Shoulder position and finger grip remain fixed.

Our changes. Two ECRL wrapping ranges are restricted to their intended path segments. The original model, derived model hashes and exact patch are retained. Attachments and joint definitions are unchanged. Unreliable supinator comparisons in pronated poses are withheld. This is an application correction, not new validation by the source authors.

Neck

Neck-model lineage by Anita Vasavada, Siping Li and Scott Delp; hyoid-model additions by Jonathan Mortensen, Anita Vasavada and Andrew Merryweather. The preserved reference model and geometry were retrieved from mjhmilla/kinematicPassengerModel.

Used here. The Neck workspace exposes six existing upper/lower cervical controls and 52 bilateral paths from the source’s 72 actuators. Hyoid paths are outside this workspace.

Our changes. No source XML, attachment, mesh or wrapping edits. OpenSim upgrades the older XML representation in memory. The torso and shoulder girdle retain their source relationships. The researcher reference file is used, not its repository’s merged passenger model.

Spine and whole body

Thoracolumbar spine and rib-cage model by Alexander Bruno, Mary Bouxsein and Dennis Anderson. The preserved full-body v11 LTpT T12Fix template and geometry come from Bern Movement Lab’s tlsm repository.

Used here. Regional Spine exposes 552 upper-body fascicles and 51 intervertebral coordinates. Whole body exposes all 598 muscle paths and 72 native joint coordinates from the same preserved template.

Our changes. No source model or mesh edits. We select controls and displayed paths, normalize some whole-body slider signs and convert geometry for display. Grouped spinal sliders distribute changes within existing joint limits; this convenience is not a published physiological coupling model. Head/neck move as one body in Whole body, and scapulae/wrists remain fixed relative to their parent segments.

Publications

  • Bruno AG, Bouxsein ML, Anderson DE. (2015). Development and Validation of a Musculoskeletal Model of the Fully Articulated Thoracolumbar Spine and Rib Cage. Journal of biomechanical engineering 137(8): 081003. DOI: 10.1115/1.4030408

Resting anatomy: Z-Anatomy and BodyParts3D

Z-Anatomy by Gauthier Kervyn, derived from BodyParts3D, © The Database Center for Life Science. Browser model prepared by hpfrei.

Used here. The anatomy catalog, selected-muscle reference panels, deep-spine atlas and deltoid reference use this resting atlas.

Our changes. Unused descriptive metadata was removed from the supplied browser GLB. Local geometry is unchanged. Muscle/bone subsets are extracted with their existing world transforms; colors, visibility, clipping and transparency are applied for display. The surfaces are not registered to the moving OpenSim body and are not deformed by its joint controls.

Tensor fasciae latae and iliotibial-tract supplement

BodyParts3D, © The Database Center for Life Science licensed under CC Attribution 4.0 International.

Used here. Right TFL, iliotibial tract, hip bone, femur, tibia and fibula from the official BodyParts3D 4.0 IS-A archive are used together as a separate resting reference.

Our changes. Original OBJ files are preserved. Conversion applies a shared proper axis rotation and millimetre-to-metre conversion, retaining vertices, topology and relative placement. Runtime color distinguishes muscle from fascia. No fitting, smoothing or pose deformation is applied.

OpenSim calculation engine

OpenSim authors and contributors, coordinated at Stanford University; Simbody and spdlog contributors. NumPy supplies supporting numerical arrays.

Used here. Native OpenSim supplies joint assembly, bone transforms, wrapped muscle–tendon paths, lengths and supported moment arms. The browser draws these results.

Our changes. Our pinned source build corrects wrap-point cache invalidation and identifies the change in its package version. No source anatomy or acceptance threshold is changed by that patch. CasADi, C3D import and the desktop visualizer are excluded. Movement Lab’s bounded longest-path search is an application feature, not a published OpenSim stretch-prescription method.
